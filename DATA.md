### Label semantics

Emotion columns are independent binaries, so a row may carry several emotions.
Most rows carry one (17,435); 3,135 carry two, 70 carry three, 2 carry four, and
38 distinct combinations occur. `Topic` always holds exactly one of 12 class
names (`Business`, `Education`, `Entertainment`, `Fashion`, `Food`, `Game`,
`Health`, `Music`, `Personal`, `Politics`, `Sports`, `Travel`) — no row has a
comma-separated topic or an empty topic.

### Business use

This data is **not suitable for production or commercial use** in its current
state:

* provenance, consent and licence are undocumented;
* there is no annotation guideline, so label semantics cannot be verified;
* visible label noise (see below);
* highly imbalanced rare classes (`Fear` 1.8%, `Surprise` 4.8%).

### Known quality issues

* **Label noise.** The audit sample includes insults and death wishes labelled
  `Sadness` rather than `Anger`. Examples in the committed CSV:
  `তোর কি মাথা খারাপ` (Sadness, Business) and `মিননির ফাঁসি চাই ...` (Sadness,
  Politics). No adjudication record exists, so it is unknown whether this is
  guideline-driven or error.
* **Duplicates.** 70 rows repeat a comment text already present.
* **Subsetting.** `ID` values reach 28,164 for 20,642 rows with all IDs unique,
  so this appears to be a subset of a larger corpus of unknown composition.
  A random split can therefore leak near-duplicate comments across
  train/validation/test; the notebooks use a label-stratified split but do not
  de-duplicate.
* **No label audit.** Precision of the labels themselves is unknown, which caps
  the meaningful accuracy of any model trained on them.

### Licensing and provenance — unresolved

The comments were scraped from YouTube, Facebook and Twitter. Those platforms'
terms of service and the original authors' copyright continue to apply, and
this repository grants no rights to redistribute them. **Nothing here
establishes a licence for the CSV.** The file is committed for research
reproducibility; it is explicitly excluded from the MIT licence in `LICENSE`.

To resolve this, someone with knowledge of the collection must record the
upstream dataset or publication, the collection date, the scraping terms in
force at that time, and either a redistribution grant or a decision to replace
the CSV with a script that regenerates it from an authorised source.
